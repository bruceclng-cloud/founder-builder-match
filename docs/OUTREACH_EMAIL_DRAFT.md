# Outreach Email Draft

## Subject

Quick hiring / getting hired research question

## Short Email

Hi [Name],

I’m exploring a project idea around matching people who are hiring for early projects with people who want to be hired, trialed, or considered for startup/product work. The idea includes fair, paid 7-day trial projects so both sides can test fit before making a bigger commitment.

I’m not trying to pitch you anything yet. I’m trying to understand whether this is a real problem from both sides.

Could I ask you a few open-ended questions? Short answers are totally fine.

### If You Have Hired Or Tried To Hire Someone

1. Have you recently tried to find a technical, product, design, growth, or operations person to help with something you’re building? What happened?
2. What made it hard to find or trust the right person?
3. If you could test working with someone through a scoped 7-day paid trial, what kind of project would feel realistic?
4. What would make that trial feel fair to both sides?
5. What budget range would feel reasonable for a small 7-day trial?
6. What would make you say no to a platform like this?

### If You Have Been Hired Or Are Looking To Be Hired

1. Are you currently looking for startup work, freelance work, portfolio work, project work, or a new role? What kind?
2. Have you ever been asked to do unpaid trial work, take-home tests, or speculative startup work? How did that feel?
3. What would make an opportunity or hiring person look serious enough to consider?
4. What minimum payment would make a scoped 7-day trial feel fair?
5. Would a verified proof-of-work record help your portfolio, LinkedIn, or job search?
6. What would make you say no to a platform like this?

No pressure if now isn’t a good time. Even a quick reply with thoughts would help.

Thanks,
Bruceng
